A. Continuous Math Derivation

1. States

  • $x(t) \geq 0$ : Position of the car. Meters (m).
  • $v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt}$ : Velocity or the car. Meters per second (m/s).
  • $a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt}$ : Acceleration of the car. Meters per second squared (m/s2).

  • $u(t) \in [0,1]$ - Analog input.

3.2. Resistance

Fresist=Frr+Fdrag=Crr⋅v+Cdrag⋅v⋅∣v∣\begin{align*} F_{\text{resist}} &= F_{\text{rr}} + F_{\text{drag}} \\ &= C_{\text{rr}} \cdot v + C_{\text{drag}} \cdot v \cdot |v| \end{align*}Fresist​​=Frr​+Fdrag​=Crr​⋅v+Cdrag​⋅v⋅∣v∣​

Where:

Where:

  • $A$ : Frontal area of the car, approx 2.2m2 here.
  • $\rho = 1.29$ kg/m3 : Air density.

3.4. Total (master ODE)

Remember Newton’s 2nd law: $ a = \frac{F}{m} $.

Fnet=m⋅a(t)=m⋅dvdtdvdt=1m(Fengine−Fresist−Fbraking)dvdt=1m(u⋅FengineMax−(Crr⋅v+Cdrag⋅v⋅∣v∣)−Fbraking(B,v))F_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a(t) = m \cdot \frac{dv}{dt} \\ \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{1}{m} \left(F_{\text{engine}} - F_{\text{resist}} - F_{\text{braking}} \right)\\ \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{1}{m} \left(u \cdot F_{\text{engineMax}} - \left(C_{\text{rr}} \cdot v + C_{\text{drag}} \cdot v \cdot |v| \right) - F_{\text{braking}}(B, v) \right)Fnet​=m⋅a(t)=m⋅dtdv​dtdv​=m1​(Fengine​−Fresist​−Fbraking​)dtdv​=m1​(u⋅FengineMax​−(Crr​⋅v+Cdrag​⋅v⋅∣v∣)−Fbraking​(B,v))

4. Constraint

For this model, we put this constraint for simplicity and preventing the car from going backwards:

If v=0 and dvdt<0, then set dvdt=0\text{If } v = 0 \text{ and } \frac{dv}{dt} < 0, \text{ then set } \frac{dv}{dt} = 0If v=0 and dtdv​<0, then set dtdv​=0
Block

it basically mean we can only accelerate, cruse, decelerate to stop, or hard stop, but we can’t go backwards, this might seem a pretty silly restriction, for video games, yeah its pretty silly, but I plan that we will approach going in reverse once we get to Model 3: Engine Torque + Gearing without Slip (1D), where we have an actual gear for going in reverse.

5.2 Scenario walkthroughs

The Master ODE is a compact statement, but it encodes many scenario in of itself. In this section, we’ll simulate specific scenarios and watch how the equation responds.

!Tips: Keep the Dashboard Open: Open Section 5.1 in another tab. We’ll reference it constantly.

Block
  • Scenario 1: The Launch (Full Throttle from Stop)
Aspect Details
Situation Car at rest ($v=0$). Driver floors the throttle ($u=1$), no brake ($B=0$).
Inputs $u=1$, $B=0$, $v=0$
Dominant Term $\frac{F_{engine}}{m}$ (Inertia is the only opponent)
ODE Says $\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{F_{max}}{m} \approx \frac{3000}{1500} = 2.0 \, \text{m/s}^2$
Real-World Feel “Pushed back in your seat” (maximum acceleration happens right at launch).

Why? At $v=0$, both resistance terms vanish: Crr⋅0=0andCdrag⋅02=0C_{rr} \cdot 0 = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad C_{drag} \cdot 0^2 = 0Crr​⋅0=0andCdrag​⋅02=0 The engine fights only mass, not air or friction.

  • Scenario 2: The Cruise (Top Speed Equilibrium)
Aspect Details
Situation Car at full throttle, has reached constant speed.
Inputs $u=1$, $B=0$, $v = v_{max}$
Dominant Term Force Balance: $F_{engine} = F_{resist}$
ODE Says $\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$ (Net force is zero)
Real-World Feel Max speed, but speedometer won’t budge.

Why? As speed increases, drag ($v^2$) grows rapidly. Eventually: Fmax=Crrvmax+Cdragvmax2F_{max} = C_{rr} v_{max} + C_{drag} v_{max}^2Fmax​=Crr​vmax​+Cdrag​vmax2​ The engine’s forward push is exactly canceled by air + road pushing back.

Analytical Bonus: Solve for $v_{max}$ directly: vmax=−Crr+Crr2+4CdragFengine2Cdragv_{max} = \frac{-C_{rr} + \sqrt{C_{rr}^2 + 4 C_{drag} F_{engine}}}{2 C_{drag}}vmax​=2Cdrag​−Crr​+Crr2​+4Cdrag​Fengine​​​ Plug in our constants: $v_{max} \approx 70 \, \text{m/s} \approx 252 \, \text{km/h}$.

  • Scenario 3: The Coast (Lifting Off at Highway Speed)
Aspect Details
Situation Car at 30 m/s (108 km/h). Driver releases throttle ($u=0$), no brake.
Inputs $u=0$, $B=0$, $v=30$
Dominant Term $-C_{drag} v^2$ (Drag dominates at high speed)
ODE Says $\frac{dv}{dt} = -\frac{1}{m}(C_{rr} \cdot 30 + C_{drag} \cdot 30^2) \approx -2.8 \, \text{m/s}^2$
Real-World Feel Rapid slowdown when you lift off at highway speeds.

Why? Drag scales with $v^2$. At 30 m/s: Fdrag=0.43⋅302≈387 NvsFrr=13⋅30=390 NF_{drag} = 0.43 \cdot 30^2 \approx 387 \, \text{N} \quad \text{vs} \quad F_{rr} = 13 \cdot 30 = 390 \, \text{N}Fdrag​=0.43⋅302≈387NvsFrr​=13⋅30=390N They’re comparable here, but drag grows much faster as speed increases.

  • Scenario 4: The Creep (Rolling to a Stop)
Aspect Details
Situation Car coasting at very low speed ($v \approx 1 \, \text{m/s}$).
Inputs $u=0$, $B=0$, $v=1$
Dominant Term $-C_{rr} v$ (Rolling resistance dominates at low speed)
ODE Says $\frac{dv}{dt} = -\frac{1}{m}(C_{rr} \cdot 1 + C_{drag} \cdot 1^2) \approx -0.009 \, \text{m/s}^2$
Real-World Feel The car seems to roll forever when creeping.

Why? At low $v$, the $v^2$ drag term becomes negligible: Cdrag⋅12=0.43 NvsCrr⋅1=13 NC_{drag} \cdot 1^2 = 0.43 \, \text{N} \quad \text{vs} \quad C_{rr} \cdot 1 = 13 \, \text{N}Cdrag​⋅12=0.43NvsCrr​⋅1=13N Rolling resistance dominates, but it’s small → tiny deceleration.

The “Infinite Stop” Quirk: Because $F_{resist} \propto v$, as $v \to 0$, force $\to 0$. Mathematically, the car asymptotically approaches zero but never reaches it in finite time. In reality, static friction and our Hard Stop Constraint (Section 4) handle the final halt. We accept this trade-off for mathematical smoothness.

  • Scenario 5: The Hard Brake (Emergency Stop)
Aspect Details
Situation Car at 30 m/s. Driver slams brakes ($B=1$), no throttle.
Inputs $u=0$, $B=1$, $v=30$
Dominant Term $-C_{braking}$ (Constant brake force dominates)
ODE Says $\frac{dv}{dt} = -\frac{1}{m}(12000 + 390 + 387) \approx -8.5 \, \text{m/s}^2 \approx 0.87g$

Why? The brake force ($12,000 \, \text{N}$) dwarfs resistance forces at most speeds. Deceleration is nearly constant until $v$ gets very low.

  • Scenario 6: The Hard Stop (At Rest, Holding Brake)
Aspect Details
Situation Car stopped ($v=0$), brake held ($B=1$).
Inputs $u=0$, $B=1$, $v=0$
Dominant Term Constraint Logic (Not the ODE itself)
ODE Says Raw: $\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$. Constraint: “If $v=0$ and force would be negative, clamp to 0.”
Real-World Feel “Car stays put at a stoplight.”

Why? At $v=0$, braking force is defined as zero (it only opposes motion). The constraint (Section 4) prevents the car from accelerating backward due to any residual negative force.

5.3 Derivable quantities

One of the most satisfying aspects of having a formal ODE is that you can calculate exact answers without running a simulation.

In this section, we’ll derive a few key performance metrics directly from the Master ODE:

Quantity What It Tells You Math Tool Required
Top Speed ($v_{max}$) Maximum achievable velocity Algebra (quadratic formula)
0→100 km/h Time Acceleration performance Integral $\int \frac{1}{a(v)} dv$
Stopping Distance Braking performance Integral $\int \frac{v}{a(v)} dv$

Why This Matters: These formulas let you tune your car analytically. Want a 10% faster top speed? The equations tell you exactly how much to increase engine force or reduce drag.

Derivable 1: Top Speed

The Question: What is the maximum speed achievable at full throttle?

The Insight: At top speed, acceleration is zero ($\frac{dv}{dt} = 0$). The engine force exactly balances resistance.

Derivation:

0=1m(Fengine−Crrvmax−Cdragvmax2)Fengine=Crrvmax+Cdragvmax2Cdragvmax2+Crrvmax−Fengine=0\begin{align*} 0 &= \frac{1}{m} \left( F_{engine} - C_{rr} v_{max} - C_{drag} v_{max}^2 \right) \\ F_{engine} &= C_{rr} v_{max} + C_{drag} v_{max}^2 \\ C_{drag} v_{max}^2 + C_{rr} v_{max} - F_{engine} &= 0 \end{align*}0Fengine​Cdrag​vmax2​+Crr​vmax​−Fengine​​=m1​(Fengine​−Crr​vmax​−Cdrag​vmax2​)=Crr​vmax​+Cdrag​vmax2​=0​
Block

This is a quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with:

  • $a = C_{drag}$
  • $b = C_{rr}$
  • $c = -F_{engine}$

Solution (positive root only, since $v \ge 0$):

vmax=−Crr+Crr2+4CdragFengine2Cdrag\boxed{ v_{max} = \frac{-C_{rr} + \sqrt{C_{rr}^2 + 4 C_{drag} F_{engine}}}{2 C_{drag}} }vmax​=2Cdrag​−Crr​+Crr2​+4Cdrag​Fengine​​​​
Block

Key Insight: Top speed depends on the square root of engine force but only linearly on drag reduction.

The Insight: Time is the integral of “inverse acceleration” over velocity.

B. Code Implementation

Now that we have a solid mental model of the continuous mathematics, we need to bridge the gap to discrete computation.

6. The discretization bridge

6.1. The core problem

Our Master ODE describes instantaneous change:

dvdt=1m(Fengine−Fresist−Fbraking(v,B))\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{1}{m} \left( F_{engine} - F_{resist} - F_{braking}(v, B) \right)dtdv​=m1​(Fengine​−Fresist​−Fbraking​(v,B))
Block

The term $\frac{dv}{dt}$ is a derivative, it asks: “What is the rate of change at this exact instant?”

Computers cannot compute “instants.” They compute steps. We need to approximate the continuous derivative using discrete time intervals.

6.2. Euler’s Method

The simplest discretization is Forward Euler Integration, if you are familiar with implementing a neural network from scratch, particularly, Gradient Descent in code, then its very similar. Here’s the derivation:

  • Step 1: Start with the derivative definition
dvdt≈vnew−voldΔt\frac{dv}{dt} \approx \frac{v_{new} - v_{old}}{\Delta t}dtdv​≈Δtvnew​−vold​​
Block

Where $\Delta t$ (often written as dt) is the time step between updates.

  • Step 2: Substitute the ODE
vnew−voldΔt=1mFnet(vold)\frac{v_{new} - v_{old}}{\Delta t} = \frac{1}{m} F_{net}(v_{old})Δtvnew​−vold​​=m1​Fnet​(vold​)
Block
  • Step 3: Solve for $v_{new}$
vnew=vold+Δt⋅Fnet(vold)mv_{new} = v_{old} + \Delta t \cdot \frac{F_{net}(v_{old})}{m}vnew​=vold​+Δt⋅mFnet​(vold​)​
Block
  • Step 4: Similarly for position
xnew=xold+Δt⋅voldx_{new} = x_{old} + \Delta t \cdot v_{old}xnew​=xold​+Δt⋅vold​
Block

The derivative $\frac{dv}{dt}$ becomes a finite difference $\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$. We’re approximating the slope over a tiny interval instead of at an exact point.

Block

6.3. Choosing timestep

The timestep is a trade-off between accuracy and performance:

Timestep Accuracy Performance Use Case
dt = 0.001 (1 ms) Excellent Expensive (1000 updates/sec) High-fidelity simulation
dt = 0.01 (10 ms) Good Reasonable (100 updates/sec) Most games
dt = 0.016 (60 Hz) Acceptable Cheap (60 updates/sec) Tied to frame rate
dt = 0.1 (100 ms) Poor Very cheap Turn-based, low precision

# === CONSTANTS (from Model 1) ===
M = 1500            # kg
F_ENGINE_MAX = 3000 # N
C_RR = 13.0         # kg/s
C_DRAG = 0.43       # kg/m
C_BRAKING = 12000   # N


class CarModel:
    def __init__(self):
        # === STATE ===
        self.x = 0.0  # position (m)
        self.v = 0.0  # velocity (m/s)

    def update(self, dt, u, B):
        """
        dt : timestep (seconds)
        u  : throttle [0, 1]
        B  : brake [0, 1] (binary)
        """

        # 1. Calculate Forces
        F_engine = u * F_ENGINE_MAX
        F_rr = C_RR * self.v
        F_drag = C_DRAG * self.v * abs(self.v)
        F_brake = C_BRAKING if (self.v > 0 and B == 1) else 0

        # 2. Net Force
        F_net = F_engine - F_rr - F_drag - F_brake

        # 3. Acceleration (Newton's 2nd Law)
        a = F_net / M

        # 4. Integrate Velocity
        self.v = self.v + dt * a

        # 5. Apply Hard Stop Constraint
        if self.v < 0:
            self.v = 0

        # 6. Integrate Position
        self.x = self.x + dt * self.v
Python

# === CONSTANTS (from Model 1) ===
M = 1500            # kg
F_ENGINE_MAX = 3000 # N
C_RR = 13.0         # kg/s
C_DRAG = 0.43       # kg/m
C_BRAKING = 12000   # N


class CarModel:
    def __init__(self):
        # === STATE ===
        self.x = 0.0  # position (m)
        self.v = 0.0  # velocity (m/s)

    def update(self, dt, u, B):
        """
        dt : timestep (seconds)
        u  : throttle [0, 1]
        B  : brake [0, 1] (binary)
        """

        # 1. Calculate Forces
        F_engine = u * F_ENGINE_MAX
        F_rr = C_RR * self.v
        F_drag = C_DRAG * self.v * abs(self.v)
        F_brake = C_BRAKING if (self.v > 0 and B == 1) else 0

        # 2. Net Force
        F_net = F_engine - F_rr - F_drag - F_brake

        # 3. Acceleration (Newton's 2nd Law)
        a = F_net / M

        # 4. Integrate Velocity
        self.v = self.v + dt * a

        # 5. Apply Hard Stop Constraint
        if self.v < 0:
            self.v = 0

        # 6. Integrate Position
        self.x = self.x + dt * self.v
