What I’ve been doing: ResQTwin

Interest of Digital Twin Technology

Most of the companies that approached me had a similar pitch: they wanted to do something that relates to digital twin, and my TrafficLab project translate almost directly: a 3D geo-referenced representation of a site (or at the very least a geo-referenced representation that can be presented on a 2D map) that is updated by real sensors, and again most related to TrafficLab, that would be existing CCTV cameras.

Geo-referenced basically mean you can point it on a map, say you have bounding boxes of cars on a road that came from an object detection model, if you can then point those cars on a map application like OpenStreetMap, then thats basically geo-referencing

Each of the companies work in completely different domain for eachother, but the demand for a digital twin updated by real sensors were common amongst all of them.

And for RQV, that would be maritime (maritime: water environment basically - beaches, rochshores, ports, even swimming pools) digital twin. And their immediate use case and environment would be New South Wales rockshores in Australia.

So in the Oceania people have this recreational sport called rockfishing, where they basically fish in the ocean, usually on rockshores, its usually more exiciting, and more dangerous compared to say fishing in a pond on a boat or something, the ocean can be relentless and unforgiving, an unexpected swell can overwhelm fisherman in a blink, knocking them off their footing, and then the force of it retreating will drag them down into the ocean and it wouldnt be just a matter of trying to get back up, it will continue to drag you down, all while sharp rocks that can even have barnacles shred your efforts of trying to get climb back.

ResQTwin: Maritime Digital Twin

So yeah basically, what I was trying to do was create a digital twin of said rockshores, updated by cameras observing the fisherman, RQV already had the tech to alert when a dangerous situation were to occur, and the digital twin’s job was to identify where that incident occured, and in the future, potentailly predicting when such an incident could occur.

Other than that, just having a digital twin of a site in general is useful, consider it a shared geospatial representation that can ingest or publish all kinds of data and have all kinds of use cases, for the maritime domain, that can be anything from SAR (search and rescue), to analytics, prediction, reporting, and of course, visualization, a picture tell a thousand words after all.

And after about 5 months, I was basically able to successfully bring the digital twin project into production, code named “ResQTwin”

So for this post, I wont really go into detail of the technicalities of ResQTwin, what I can say that it is v0, it seems to have been working well enough for v0, and I will definitely be continuing to improve it to match my evolving standard, and of course, the requirement of the customer as well
